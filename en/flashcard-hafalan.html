How to make flashcards for effective memorization

Turn your notes into question cards: one card, one question, answer before you flip.

Why flashcards help memorization

Your brain remembers better when you actively answer instead of just re-reading. Flashcards force you to guess the answer first — that guessing process is what strengthens memory.

Turn notes into questions

  1. Take one topic or one page of notes.
  2. Write one question per card, for example “What is the formula for the area of a circle?” — not “Chapter 3”.
  3. Write a short, clear answer.
  4. In Rancana, each card side is limited to 70 characters. Split a long answer into several cards.

Write in your own words

Do not copy your notes word for word. Rephrasing in your own words makes the material stick and easier to recall during a test.

Practise answer-first

  1. Shuffle the cards so you do not memorize their order.
  2. Read the question, then answer out loud or write it down first.
  3. Flip the card and check your answer.
  4. Set aside the cards you got wrong to review tomorrow.

Repeat the next day

Memorization needs repetition. Review yesterday's wrong cards, then add a few new ones. A short 10–15 minute session with the study timer is enough for one round.

Keep going with the same subject

Connect your classes, assignments, and practice cards through the same subject.
